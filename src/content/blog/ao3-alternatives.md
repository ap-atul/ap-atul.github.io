> This is part two of a three-part series. Part one covers [what happened to AO3](/blog/ao3-down-what-happened).

AO3 is offline after a security incident, and while its content is safe, you probably still want something to read tonight. No single site replaces AO3. Its tagging system, the lack of ads and the "don't like, don't read" philosophy are genuinely unique. But a mix of the sites below will get you through.

Here's an honest rundown of each one: what it's good at, where it falls short, and who it suits.

## 1. fanfiction.net (ffn)

**Best for:** older fandoms, classic fics, huge back catalogues.

[FanFiction.Net](https://www.fanfiction.net) is the grandparent of fanfic archives, online since 1998. Many fics that later moved to AO3 still have their original copies here, especially in Harry Potter, Naruto, Pokémon and anything popular in the 2000s and early 2010s.

**Good:**
- millions of works, many cross-posted with AO3
- the search and filter by fandom, rating, language and completion status work fine
- it's stable and has been around for decades

**Not so good:**
- far weaker tagging than AO3, with only up to four characters and two genres
- no explicit (MA) content allowed under its rules
- ads, and a dated interface

**Tip:** if a favourite AO3 author has been writing for a while, search their username on FFN. There's a good chance their older works live here too.

## 2. wattpad

**Best for:** mobile readers, original fiction, younger fandoms (K-pop, YouTubers, anime).

[Wattpad](https://www.wattpad.com) publicly offered support to AO3 readers during the outage. It has a polished app and an enormous audience.

**Good:**
- excellent mobile app with offline reading
- huge amount of original fiction alongside fanfic
- in-line comments make it very social

**Not so good:**
- discovery is driven by an algorithm, not tags, so finding niche ships is harder
- ads and paid "Wattpad Originals" content
- stricter content rules than AO3

## 3. tumblr

**Best for:** following specific authors, short fics, prompt fills, and drabbles.

A lot of writers have started posting new chapters on [Tumblr](https://www.tumblr.com) during the outage, and Tumblr's own account invited people to share their favourite fics. If you follow writers rather than tags, this is where to look first.

**Good:** direct contact with authors, fast updates, strong fandom culture.

**Not so good:** terrible for long fics, and the search is unreliable. Use it to find out *where* an author is posting, not as a reading platform.

## 4. dreamwidth and livejournal communities

**Best for:** older and smaller fandoms, fic exchanges, community archives.

Before AO3 existed, a huge amount of fandom lived on LiveJournal, and much of it moved to [Dreamwidth](https://www.dreamwidth.org). Many fic communities and exchange archives are still active there. Fanlore is a great way to find which community hosts which fandom.

## 5. spacebattles, sufficient velocity and questionable questing

**Best for:** crossovers, self-inserts, worm fics, "what if" stories, long-running serials.

These forums host a massive amount of long, very active fanfiction, much of it updated weekly in threadmarked stories. The tone leans towards action, worldbuilding and power fantasy, so it's a different vibe from AO3, but the quality at the top end is excellent.

## 6. the wayback machine

**Best for:** finding one specific fic you can't live without.

The [Internet Archive's Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org) saved snapshots of many AO3 pages over the years. Paste in an AO3 work URL (for example `archiveofourown.org/works/12345`) and you may find a saved copy.

**Caveats:**
- it has been very slow under the extra traffic during the outage
- multi-chapter works are often only partly saved
- please be gentle with it, because it's a non-profit too

## 7. community drives and the r/ao3 megathreads

The fandom has organised itself impressively. The **r/AO3 subreddit** has megathreads where readers share downloaded EPUBs and PDFs, and public Google Drive collections such as the "Fanfic Emergency Fund" have collected works across many fandoms.

A note on etiquette: these files are people's creative work. Download what you need, don't re-upload works to sites for profit, and respect it if an author asks for their fic to be removed from shared folders.

## what to avoid

**Unofficial "AO3 mirror" sites.** During the outage, at least one AI-generated site scraped fanfiction from across the internet and tried to sell audiobook versions of it. It was taken down after a backlash. Sites like this take authors' work without permission, and some may be phishing for AO3 logins. **Never enter your AO3 username or password anywhere except `archiveofourown.org`.**

**"Verify your AO3 account" emails.** These are scams. AO3 can't email you to verify anything while it's offline.

## quick comparison

| site | explicit content | tagging | mobile app | best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FanFiction.Net | no | basic | yes | older fandoms |
| Wattpad | limited | basic | excellent | mobile, original fic |
| Tumblr | limited | poor search | yes | following authors |
| Dreamwidth | yes | community-based | no | small fandoms, exchanges |
| SpaceBattles / SV | SV/QQ only | threadmarks | no | long serials, crossovers |
| Wayback Machine | as archived | none | no | one specific fic |

## my take

For most people, the practical setup is **FFN for discovery, Tumblr to follow your favourite authors, and the r/AO3 megathreads for specific fics**. Then go back to AO3 when it returns. It will, and your bookmarks will be waiting.
