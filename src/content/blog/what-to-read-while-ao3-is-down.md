> This is part three of a three-part series. See [what happened to AO3](/blog/ao3-down-what-happened) and [the best AO3 alternatives](/blog/ao3-alternatives).

If AO3 was your nightly reading habit, the outage leaves a real hole. This post has two goals: give you good things to read **right now**, and help you set things up so that next time a site goes down, your favourite stories are already on your device.

## part 1: what to read right now

### if you love long, slow-burn fic → web novels

Fanfic readers tend to love **long, serialised, character-driven stories**. That's exactly what web novels are.

- **[Royal Road](https://www.royalroad.com)** is the biggest English-language home for original web serials: fantasy, progression fantasy, LitRPG, slice of life and more. It has tags and a "follow" system that will feel familiar to AO3 readers. Look at the "Best Rated" and "Complete" lists to start.
- **[Scribble Hub](https://www.scribblehub.com)** is similar, with a stronger lean towards anime-style stories and a very active community.
- **Webnovel and translated Chinese, Korean and Japanese novels.** Cultivation, isekai and romance-fantasy series run for hundreds or thousands of chapters. If you liked "long fic with 300k+ words", you'll be at home.

### if you love the fandom side → read the source

Use the break to read the thing your fandom is actually based on, or a sequel you skipped. A lot of AO3's biggest fandoms are adapted from **light novels, manga and manhwa**, and the originals often have extra material the adaptation cut.

### if you love tropes → find published books with them

Many AO3 tropes have whole published genres behind them:

- **enemies-to-lovers / slow burn** → romantasy
- **found family** → cosy fantasy
- **time loop / fix-it fic** → time-loop and "regression" novels
- **coffee shop AU** → cosy romance and cosy fantasy
- **canon divergence** → alternate-history fiction

Ask your local library about Libby or OverDrive. You can borrow ebooks and audiobooks for free with a library card.

### if you just want *your* fics → check what you already have

Look through your downloads folder, your phone's Files app or your Google Play Books library. A lot of people have downloaded AO3 EPUBs over the years and forgotten about them.

## part 2: never lose your library again

The real lesson of this outage is that **an online archive is not a backup**. AO3 has a reputation for being solid, and this still happened. Here's how to protect yourself.

### 1. use ao3's built-in download button

Every work on AO3 has a **Download** button with **EPUB, MOBI, AZW3, PDF and HTML** options. When the site comes back:

- download your **all-time favourites first**
- choose **EPUB** for most reading apps, or **AZW3** for Kindle
- for multi-chapter works in progress, download again after big updates

### 2. go through your bookmarks while you remember

Your AO3 bookmarks page is effectively your reading list. After the site returns, set aside an evening, go through your bookmarks and download the ones you would truly miss. Even 20–30 key fics will make the next outage much less painful.

### 3. automate it with calibre + fanficfare

For a larger library:

- **[Calibre](https://calibre-ebook.com)** is a free ebook manager for Windows, macOS and Linux.
- **FanFicFare** is a Calibre plugin that downloads fics from AO3, FFN and many other archives, and can **update** stories when new chapters come out.

Together they give you a searchable, offline library of everything you love. Please be polite with automated downloading, though. Don't bulk-scrape thousands of works, especially in the days after AO3 returns while it's under heavy load.

### 4. keep your files in two places

A backup on one laptop is one spilled coffee away from disappearing. Keep a copy in a cloud folder (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud) **and** on a device or external drive.

### 5. pick a good offline reading app

- **Android:** Moon+ Reader, ReadEra or Google Play Books (upload your EPUBs)
- **iOS:** Apple Books or KyBook
- **Kindle:** send AZW3/EPUB files with "Send to Kindle"
- **Desktop:** Calibre's built-in viewer or Thorium Reader

If you read web novels or manga on Android, I also make a couple of free apps, [Ranobe](https://github.com/ranobe-org/ranobe) for light novels and MangaD for manga, which both download chapters for offline reading. They're built for exactly this "I want it on my phone, not on someone's server" mindset.

## part 3: support the creators

When AO3 returns:

- **leave kudos and comments** on the fics that got you through. Writers have been worried about their work too
- **consider donating to the OTW** if you can. AO3 runs on volunteers and donations, and recovering from an incident like this is expensive
- **follow authors** on whichever other platforms they use, so you can find them if something like this happens again

## a final checklist

- [ ] changed any password I reused from AO3
- [ ] turned on two-factor sign-in for my email
- [ ] found something to read (Royal Road, FFN, the source material, the library)
- [ ] after AO3 returns: changed my AO3 password
- [ ] after AO3 returns: downloaded my top favourites as EPUB
- [ ] backed up my library in two places

AO3 will come back, and your bookmarks and kudos are safe. But stories you love are worth keeping a copy of, and now is a good time to start.
